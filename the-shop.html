The Shop

A 2,600 square-foot shop in the Greater Cincinnati area, built in 1996 and in continuous operation since. What follows is a walk through what's on the floor — organized by class of machine — along with a look at some of the parts that have come off it.

Shop & tooling

The shop is one open room — modest, well-organized, lit from the windows and overhead. Drawers of cutters, drills, taps, and reamers line the walls, kept close to the machines that use them.

CNC machines

A CNC mill and lathe for the work that calls for it — repeatable runs, tighter features, and contours that are hard to hold by hand. They sit alongside the manual machines and pick up the jobs they suit.

Milling machines

Vertical knee-mills handle the bulk of milling work — squaring, slotting, facing, drilling, boring, and the everyday work of bringing a piece of stock to print. Good for one-offs and small runs alike.

Grinders

Surface and tool grinders for finishing flat work and sharpening cutters. Where milling and turning leave a part, grinding takes it the rest of the way to tolerance and finish.

Saws

Horizontal and vertical bandsaws for cutting bar stock and plate to rough size — the first step on most jobs that come in.

Sanders

Belt and disc sanders for deburring, easing edges, and bringing a part to its final finish after the machining is done.

Welding & fabrication

MIG, TIG, and arc welding for joining machined parts into larger assemblies, and for fabricating brackets, frames, and weldments to print.

Inspection

A coordinate measuring machine, an optical comparator, gauge blocks, micrometers, height gauges, and the everyday measuring tools that confirm a finished part matches the drawing.

Electrical

Control panels, wiring, and the electrical work that goes into the machines built here — laid out and assembled in-house.

Robotics

Automation built into the custom machinery — robotic handling and motion designed and integrated alongside the mechanical work.

Machinery built

Custom equipment and assemblies built for specific jobs — the kind of one-off machinery, designed and built in-house, that doesn't have a part number in any catalog.

Wood working

A wood-working corner for pattern work, jigs, fixtures, crating, and the occasional finished piece — for the times when metal isn't what the job calls for.

Parts & fixtures

A sampling of finished work — machined parts, fixtures, and small assemblies that have come off the floor over the years. The drawing and the material change; the standard doesn't.

CNC parts

Parts turned and milled on the CNC machines — features and finishes that lend themselves to programmed cuts and repeatable runs.

Miscellaneous

Drill presses, an air compressor, hand tools, tooling, and the smaller equipment that supports the larger machines.
